**Character Concept: Lord Auberon "Aubrey" Fitzroy-Hamilton**

**Playbook:** The Monstrous

**High Concept:** Fae-blooded aristocrat from a fallen monster-hunting dynasty, living in reduced circumstances in Holywell while literally feeding off the admiration of the local community.

**Background:** Descendant of Major Lord Augustus Fitzroy-Hamilton of the 13th Ethereal Dragoons (Victorian monster hunters). The family bloodline was mixed with fae heritage generations ago, leading to both their success at monster hunting and eventual decline. Now lives in what locals call "the big house" (modest by aristocratic standards but still impressive to neighbors).

**Monstrous Build:**

- **Curse:** Feed (attention/admiration) - Must have regular admiration from others to sustain themselves
- **Natural Attacks:** Glamour (magical force with charm/mesmerizing effects)
- **Moves:** Mental Dominion, Incorporeal

**Additional Fae Traits:**

- **Iron Sensitivity:** Gets sneezy/itchy around iron (like hay fever) - explains their preference for silver cutlery and antique brass fixtures
- **Compelled by Courtesy:** Cannot refuse offered food/drink without good reason, must return favours (though can interpret creatively)
- **Name Sensitivity:** Guards their full true name carefully, prefers "Aubrey" partly for this reason

**Role in HNWA:** Positions themselves as the local expert on supernatural matters (which they genuinely are), hosting meetings at their home. Uses their aristocratic bearing and fae charisma to maintain their position as a central figure in the community, which conveniently feeds their supernatural needs.

**Tone:** Comedic elements come from the contrast between supernatural needs and aristocratic pretensions, attempting to maintain dignity while navigating modern life with fae limitations. Think Buffy/Jonathan Creek level of supernatural comedy rather than slapstick.

**Lord Auberon Maelgwyn Peregrine Silverthorne Fitzroy-Hamilton**

Breaking it down:

- **Auberon** - Shakespeare's fairy king
- **Maelgwyn** - Welsh for "blessed prince" (and sounds properly ancient)
	- Pronounced MILE-gwin (gwin rhymes with win)
- **Peregrine** - means "wanderer" or "pilgrim" (very fae)
- **Silverthorne** - hints at both fae nature and the family's monster-hunting legacy (silver being useful against many supernatural creatures)
- **Fitzroy-Hamilton** - the established family name

The "MILE-gwin PEAR-eh-grin" creates that lovely bouncing cadence in the middle of this imposing name. It actually adds to the fae quality - fairy names in folklore often have that sing-song quality to them. And it makes it even more understandable why he'd want to keep this full name hidden; it practically hums with magical significance!

He'd be absolutely mortified if anyone learned his full name, believing it gives power over him in the fae tradition. He insists on "Aubrey" or at most "Lord Auberon" in formal settings. The full name would only appear on ancient family documents or if someone did some serious digging into the family records.

The name also has that wonderful aristocratic quality where it just keeps going, which adds to both the comedy and the sense of ancient lineage. Anyone who discovers it might assume the length is just aristocratic pretension, not realizing each name has mystical significance!
